# EVIDENCE TO LOOK FOR (all must be verified before citing; I have not confirmed any of these)

Candidate studies to locate and read (check each exists, and that the findings match what you write):

- **CREDO (Stanford)** national charter school studies (2013 and 2023 editions) and any RI-specific or Northeast report: matched-student comparisons.
- **Abdulkadiroglu, Angrist, Dynarski, Kane, Pathak (2011), Quarterly Journal of Economics**: Boston charter lotteries.
- **Angrist, Pathak, Walters (2013), AEJ: Applied**: why urban charters outperform non-urban ones.
- **Cohodes, Setren, Walters**: replication of successful Boston charter models (expansion question, directly relevant to "doubling").
- **Dobbie and Fryer**: Harlem Children's Zone and Promise Academy.
- Research on **fiscal effects on districts** and **competitive effects on district schools** (e.g. Cohodes and Parham review; Ridley and Terrier on fiscal externalities).
- Research on **teachers in charters** (turnover, pay, unionization).
- RI-specific: RIDE reports, Rhode Island Kids Count, local think tank or press analyses on funding formula impacts (treat as secondary and check claims against RIDE data).

**Key methodological point for the memo and appendix:** lottery (oversubscribed admissions) studies give the cleanest effect of attending a charter versus not; matched-student and value-added studies are weaker; raw proficiency gaps reflect selection. Be explicit about which evidence you lean on and why, and about whether lottery results (from over-subscribed, mostly urban schools) generalize to doubling the cap.

**Own-analysis ideas (state-level, observational):**

1. School-level charter vs. district-comparison proficiency, with controls for demographics (descriptive, not causal).
2. Growth or cohort-based measures from SEDA, which are less distorted by starting point than proficiency levels.
3. District-level exposure: charter share of enrollment vs. district enrollment change and per-pupil spending.
4. Scenario arithmetic: effect size × students shifted × baseline = statewide change (with ranges).
